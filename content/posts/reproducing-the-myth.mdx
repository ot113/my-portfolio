This is my graduation thesis for the Department of History at METU, written under the supervision of Assoc. Prof. Dr. Bahar Gürsel. It reads the *Mafia* games as historical texts and measures them against what we actually know about the Sicilian and Italian-American mafia.

**[Read the full thesis (PDF)](/files/Oguzcan_Taskin_Graduation_Thesis.pdf)**

## Thesis statement

This thesis argues that the Mafia video game franchise distorts the documented history of the Sicilian and Italian-American mafia not by inventing falsehoods but through selective framing: the games preserve genuine fragments of the historical record, yet reframe them so that the organization's documented reality as a modern, economically driven, and politically embedded protection business recedes, and the mafia's own ideological self-image, the myth of "rustic chivalry," takes its place. This substitution occurs because the romance of honour, loyalty, and ancient Sicilian roots is more marketable and more immersive than the bureaucratic and economic reality the sources describe. The games are therefore neither faithful history nor pure fiction, but a particular kind of fiction that operates through identifiable and recurring strategies of distortion, traced here across three games depicting three eras of mafia history.

## What it covers

- **Historical background:** where the myth of "rustic chivalry" comes from, and why the historical record, anchored here in John Dickie's *Cosa Nostra*, contradicts it.
- **Mafia:** the pre-war Italian-American mafia.
- **Mafia II:** the post-war era.
- **Mafia: The Old Country:** the Sicilian roots of the organization.
- **Conclusion:** the recurring strategies of distortion across the franchise, and why reading historical games carefully matters for the discipline of history.

## Why it is here

Games are participatory. A film shows you a gangster living by a code; a game asks you to live by it yourself. That is exactly where design choices start to carry historical claims, and it is the same question I care about as a designer: what a game tells the player, and what it lets them believe.
